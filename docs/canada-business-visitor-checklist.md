# Canada — Business Travel Document Requirements

*Reference notes. Last verified 8 September 2026. General information, not legal
advice — confirm every item against IRCC before you file or fly.*

---

> **⚠️ Time-sensitive as of September 2026.** Two US-side changes dominate any
> Canada trip taken from the United States on a nonimmigrant status:
> third-country visa stamping in Canada effectively ended **6 Sep 2025**, and the
> **duration-of-status → fixed-admission-period** rule takes effect
> **15 Sep 2026**. See
> [You are in the US on a nonimmigrant status](#you-are-in-the-us-on-a-nonimmigrant-status-f-1-optstem-opt-h-1b-l-1-o-1-tn).

## First: Canada has no "B-1 visa"

**B-1 is a United States classification.** Canada has no equivalent letter code
and no "business visa." If you search for a Canadian B-1 you will find US-visa
content or scraper sites selling a product that does not exist.

The Canadian equivalent of B-1 business travel is **entry as a *business
visitor*** — a temporary resident admitted under
[IRPR s. 186(a)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-227/section-186.html)
and defined at [s. 187](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-227/section-187.html),
who does business in Canada **without a work permit**.

That produces two independent questions, and you have to answer both:

| | Question | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Do my *activities* qualify as a business visitor? | Whether you need a **work permit** |
| **2** | What is my *nationality / status*? | Whether you need an **eTA**, a **visitor visa (TRV)**, or neither |

They are unrelated. A US citizen doing work-permit-level work needs a permit
despite needing no visa. An Indian national attending a two-day meeting needs a
TRV despite needing no permit.

## Question 1 — Do you qualify as a business visitor?

### The three tests (IRPR 187(2)–(3))

All three must hold:

1. **No entry into the Canadian labour market** — you are not taking a job a
   Canadian could hold.
2. **Activity is international in scope** — a cross-border business purpose, not
   domestic Canadian operations.
3. **Money stays offshore** — your primary source of remuneration is outside
   Canada, *and* your employer's principal place of business and actual place of
   accrual of profits remain predominantly outside Canada.

### Activities that normally qualify

- Meetings, consultations, negotiations, discussions
- Conferences, conventions, trade shows, professional events
- Site visits, facility tours, research and fact-finding
- Soliciting business / selling to businesses (**not** to the general public)
- Purchasing Canadian goods or services for a foreign employer, or receiving
  training on a purchase
- Receiving or giving training within a Canadian parent, subsidiary or affiliate
  of the foreign company that employs you
- **After-sales / after-lease service** — installing, inspecting, repairing,
  servicing or supervising work on equipment sold or leased by a company outside
  Canada, *where the original sales or lease contract provides for that service*.
  This does **not** cover hands-on construction or building trades work.

### Activities that require a work permit instead

Secretarial, managerial, technical or production work; hands-on operational
duties; being paid by or embedded into a Canadian entity; **or any stay longer
than six months.** If you find yourself explaining why what you'll be doing is
"technically not work," you probably need a permit.

### ⚠️ March 2026 — the bar for evidence went up

On **19 March 2026** IRCC published updated instructions to officers on business
visitor eligibility. Two things changed in practice:

- **In your favour:** the three statutory examples in s. 187(2) are confirmed as
  *examples only*, not an exhaustive list — similar business purposes can
  qualify.
- **Against you:** officers are told to scrutinise the three tests harder, and
  the onus is explicitly on the traveller. Expect to be asked to *prove* foreign
  employment, foreign-source pay, genuine international scope, and temporary
  intent — not merely assert them.

Practical effect: carry paper. See [What to carry at the border](#question-3--what-to-carry-at-the-port-of-entry).

## Question 2 — Which entry document do you need?

| Your status | Need | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Canadian citizen / PR** | — | Canadian passport or PR card |
| **US citizen** | **Nothing** | Valid US passport. No eTA, no visa — by air, land or sea |
| **US lawful permanent resident (green card)** | **Nothing** | eTA-exempt since 26 Apr 2022. Must carry **valid passport + physical green card** |
| **Visa-exempt national** (UK, EU/Schengen, Australia, Japan, South Korea, etc.) | **eTA** | Only when arriving **by air**. Not required by land or sea |
| **Partially visa-exempt** (see below) | **eTA or TRV** | eTA only if flying **and** you hold a valid US non-immigrant visa **or** held a Canadian TRV in the last 10 years. Otherwise TRV |
| **Everyone else** — incl. **India**, China, Nigeria, Pakistan, Vietnam | **TRV (visitor visa)** | Application IMM 5257. This is the long path — plan for it |

### Partially visa-exempt ("partial eTA") countries

Roughly 17 as of 2026: Antigua and Barbuda, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Costa
Rica, Indonesia, Malaysia, Morocco, Panama, Philippines, St. Kitts and Nevis,
St. Lucia, St. Vincent and the Grenadines, Seychelles, Thailand, Trinidad and
Tobago, Uruguay. Indonesia and Malaysia were added in **May 2026**.

**India is not on this list.** An Indian passport holder needs a TRV regardless
of holding a valid US visa.

> ⚠️ The list changes without much notice. Confirm yours on IRCC's
> [Find out if you need a visa](https://www.cic.gc.ca/english/visit/visas.asp)
> tool before assuming anything here still holds.

### eTA vs TRV at a glance

| | eTA | TRV (visitor visa) |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | CAD $7 | CAD $100 (+ $85 biometrics) |
| Where | Online, minutes | Online / VAC, weeks to months |
| Biometrics | No | Yes, most applicants |
| Validity | 5 years or passport expiry | Up to passport expiry, often less |
| Applies to | Air travel only | Any mode of entry |

## The TRV document checklist (visa-required travellers)

Applying online generates a **personalised** checklist in the IRCC portal —
that one is authoritative. This is what a business-purpose application typically
contains.

### Forms

- **IMM 5257** — Application for Visitor Visa (Temporary Resident Visa). Validate
  the form so the barcode page generates
- **IMM 5645** or **IMM 5707** — Family Information (which one depends on the
  visa office)
- **IMM 5476** — Use of a Representative, if a lawyer/consultant is filing
- **IMM 5484** — Document Checklist, for paper applications

### Identity and status

- Passport — valid well past your travel dates, with blank pages. Copies of the
  bio page and every visa, entry stamp and extension
- Digital photo to IRCC specification (paper applications: 35 × 45 mm, taken
  within 6 months)
- **Proof of status where you live, if you are not a citizen there** — e.g. for
  someone applying from the US: I-797 approval notice, I-94 record, valid US
  visa stamp, or green card

### Purpose of travel — the part that decides a business application

- **Invitation letter from the Canadian host** — the single most important
  document. It should state:
  - your full name, date of birth, employer and job title
  - the host company's details and **24-hour contact** for the trip
  - the business relationship between the two companies
  - specific purpose and agenda of the visit
  - exact dates and duration
  - who pays which expenses
  - **explicit confirmation that you remain employed and paid outside Canada and
    will receive no funds from the Canadian company**
- **Employer support letter from your own (foreign) employer** — your role,
  salary, length of service, approved leave dates, purpose of trip, confirmation
  you return to your job, and who bears the cost
- Supporting evidence as applicable: conference or trade-show registration,
  meeting agenda, purchase orders, contracts, **warranty or service agreement**
  if you're travelling for after-sales service, letters from a parent company

Ask the Canadian host for **two originals** of the invitation letter: one for
the visa application, one to carry at the border.

### Financial and ties

- Bank statements — typically 4–6 months
- Recent pay stubs, employment letter, tax returns
- Evidence you will leave: ongoing employment, property, family, return travel
  plan, prior travel history and clean immigration record

### Travel plan

- Itinerary and intended accommodation
- **Do not buy non-refundable tickets before approval** — IRCC says so explicitly,
  and a booked ticket does not accelerate anything

### Biometrics

Fingerprints and photo, required for most applicants aged **14–79**. Fee paid
with the application; you attend a VAC after receiving the Biometrics
Instruction Letter. Valid **10 years** — if you gave biometrics for a previous
Canadian application within that window, check whether you're exempt.

### Fees and timelines (2026)

| Item | Fee (CAD) |
|---|---|
| Visitor visa, per person | $100 |
| Family (up to 5) | $500 max |
| Biometrics, per person | $85 |
| Biometrics, family | $170 max |
| eTA | $7 |

Processing from India in 2026 has run roughly **4 weeks to 12 weeks**, with
post-biometrics waits of 8–12 weeks widely reported. Treat IRCC's published
estimate as a median, not a commitment, and **start at least 3 months out** for
a fixed business date. Medical exams and police certificates are not normally
required for visits under six months.

## Question 3 — What to carry at the port of entry

A visa or eTA only lets you *travel to* Canada. **The CBSA officer at the border
decides admissibility**, and can ask for anything above. After the March 2026
instructions this is where business trips actually fail.

Carry in hand luggage — never checked:

- [ ] Passport with valid eTA/TRV (or passport + green card, or US passport)
- [ ] Invitation letter from the Canadian host (original)
- [ ] Employer support letter confirming foreign employment and pay
- [ ] Recent pay stub — the fastest proof of foreign-source remuneration
- [ ] Meeting agenda, conference registration, or contract for the visit
- [ ] Return ticket
- [ ] Host's 24-hour contact details
- [ ] Warranty/service contract, if travelling for after-sales service

Be ready to say in one sentence what you're doing, who pays you, and when you
leave. Answer that consistently with your documents.

## Special cases

### You are in the US on a nonimmigrant status (F-1 OPT/STEM OPT, H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN)

Your US status does **not** determine what Canada requires — your **citizenship**
does. An Indian national on F-1 STEM OPT or H-1B still needs a **TRV**. There is
no shortcut, and a valid US visa does not substitute (India is not a partial-eTA
country).

The Canadian side of such a trip is usually the *easy* half. The hard half is
getting back into the United States.

#### Canada is satisfied by US employment

For the business visitor tests, "outside Canada" means outside Canada — US-based
employment and US-source pay satisfy the remuneration and profit-accrual tests
cleanly. Use your US employer's letter and a US pay stub as the proof.

One caveat specific to nonimmigrants: IRCC also weighs whether you will *leave*
Canada. Your tie is your US job and valid US status. If your US status is thin
at the time you apply — EAD close to expiry, no valid US visa stamp, a pending
change of status — that weakens the file, because an officer can reasonably ask
whether you'd be able to return to the US at all. Apply while your US documents
have runway.

#### Practical friction: you surrender your passport

After a TRV is approved, IRCC issues a passport request letter and you mail the
passport to a VAC for the visa foil. **You will not have your passport for a
stretch of weeks.** Plan for that: don't schedule other international travel, and
don't let it collide with any US filing that needs the document.

#### Automatic visa revalidation (AVR) — and why its safety margin is gone

If your **US visa stamp has expired** but your status is valid, AVR normally lets
you re-enter the US after a trip of **30 days or less to Canada or Mexico only**.

For **F-1 students on OPT / STEM OPT**, AVR requires:

- [ ] Valid, unexpired **passport**
- [ ] Valid **EAD card** (the OPT or STEM OPT card)
- [ ] **I-20 endorsed for travel by your DSO** — on OPT the travel signature is
      valid **6 months**, not 12. Check the date
- [ ] Unexpired **I-94**
- [ ] Trip **≤ 30 days**, to **Canada only** — no third country, not even a
      layover or a connecting flight through one
- [ ] You did **not** apply for a US visa while in Canada
- [ ] You are not a national of a designated state sponsor of terrorism
      (India is not one)

> ### ⚠️ The fallback closed on 6 September 2025
>
> AVR was historically low-risk because a failure was recoverable — you renewed
> the stamp in Toronto or Montreal and came home.
>
> **That is no longer available.** Effective **6 September 2025**, the US State
> Department requires nonimmigrant visa interviews in the applicant's **country
> of nationality or residence**, ending routine third-country stamping in Canada
> and Mexico. An Indian national refused AVR at the border can no longer simply
> renew in Canada — the realistic path is flying to **India**, waiting for an
> appointment, and hoping for no administrative processing.
>
> And note the trap: **applying for a US visa in Canada voids AVR.** Once you
> apply, you cannot fall back on it. There is no second attempt.
>
> AVR is still low-*probability* of failure. It is now very high-*cost* of
> failure. Those are different risks, and only the second one matters when you
> have a job in the US.

CBP discretion is also now explicit: as part of the September 2026 rulemaking,
the admission language moved from "shall" to "**may**." Meeting every criterion
has never guaranteed admission, and the regulation now says so.

**If your F-1 stamp is still valid and covers your return date, none of the
above applies to you** — travel on the stamp and this is an ordinary trip.

#### ⚠️ Timing: the D/S rule takes effect 15 September 2026

DHS's final rule replacing **duration of status (D/S)** with **fixed admission
periods** for F, J and I nonimmigrants takes effect **15 September 2026**. Two
consequences for anyone on OPT or STEM OPT:

1. **Be physically inside the US on 15 September 2026.** Students in F-1 status
   and present in the US on that date are grandfathered: status continues to the
   later of the I-20 program end date or the **STEM OPT EAD expiry** (capped at
   four years). A trip that straddles the date forfeits that treatment for no
   reason.
2. **Re-entry on or after 15 September 2026 is on a date-specific I-94**, not
   D/S — a fixed admission period plus a short grace period, generally keyed to
   your EAD end date. This matters later, not at the border: if you subsequently
   file anything (an H-1B change of status, a further OPT action) and your I-94
   expires while it is pending, filing the underlying petition alone no longer
   protects your stay — you need **Form I-539** extension of stay alongside it.

Transition relief exists for OPT/STEM OPT applications timely filed **on or
before 18 March 2027** (no EOS required), but that covers *applying* for STEM
OPT, not travel by someone already on it.

Given a TRV takes weeks, travel before 15 September 2026 is unrealistic unless
you already hold a valid Canadian visa. Plan on returning under the new regime.

#### What to carry for US re-entry on STEM OPT

- [ ] Passport valid 6+ months
- [ ] F-1 visa stamp — or the AVR document set above
- [ ] **STEM OPT EAD card**
- [ ] **I-20 with a travel signature under 6 months old**, showing the STEM OPT
      recommendation
- [ ] **Employment verification letter** — employer name, E-Verify participation,
      job title, start date, that the role relates to your degree
- [ ] Recent **pay stubs**
- [ ] Copy of your signed **Form I-983** training plan
- [ ] Degree certificate / transcript
- [ ] DSO contact details, including after-hours

CBP can and does question OPT students about employment, since unemployment days
count against status (90 days on post-completion OPT, 150 total with the STEM
extension — travel does **not** pause the clock). Have the employment proof in
hand, not in email.

#### Before you book anything

1. **Talk to your DSO first.** They issue the travel signature and will have a
   current institutional position on travel risk — several international offices
   now advise against relying on AVR at all.
2. **If you have an employer immigration team, route it through them.** A large
   employer will have a standing policy on OPT-holder travel and will pay for
   counsel. Use it.
3. **Ask whether the trip is actually necessary.** For a conference or an
   internal meeting, remote attendance or sending a colleague with a US passport
   costs nothing. Weigh that honestly against a scenario where you are in Toronto
   with an expired stamp and no route home.

### US or Mexican citizens — CUSMA

CUSMA (formerly NAFTA) **Chapter 16, Appendix 1, Section B** lists business
visitor categories: research and design, growth/manufacture/production,
marketing, sales, distribution, after-sales service, and general service. Same
three tests apply — international scope, no Canadian labour market entry,
offshore pay and profits. Stay capped at six months.

CUSMA covers **citizens** of the US and Mexico only — **not** permanent
residents or visa holders of those countries.

### Trip is longer than six months, or the work is hands-on

Stop treating it as a business visit. That is a work permit question, and
possibly an LMIA or LMIA-exempt (CUSMA professional, intra-company transferee)
question. Get counsel.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Searching for a "Canada B-1 visa" | Search **business visitor** + **eTA/TRV** |
| Assuming a valid US visa gets you in | Only for partial-eTA countries, by air. Not India |
| Weak or generic invitation letter | Named individual, specific agenda, explicit "paid outside Canada, no Canadian funds" |
| No proof of foreign-source pay | Carry a recent pay stub *and* the employer letter |
| Booking non-refundable travel before approval | Book after approval |
| Documents in checked baggage | Hand luggage |
| Activity is really work | Apply for a work permit |
| Applying 3 weeks before the meeting | Start 3 months out |
| On OPT, relying on AVR with an expired stamp | Understand the fallback closed Sep 2025 — talk to your DSO |
| Travel signature over 6 months old | On OPT it's 6 months, not 12. Re-sign before you go |
| Trip straddling 15 Sep 2026 | Be in the US on that date |
