# Canada Business-Visitor Visa — Documents & Step-by-Step Application

**The "B-1 equivalent" for Canada: a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV) for business
purposes, filed online from the United States.**

> **Profile this guide is written for:** Indian citizen, physically in the US on
> **F-1 STEM OPT**, travelling to Canada for business (meetings, conference, site
> visit, after-sales service). If that isn't you, the checklist still applies but
> the US-status and re-entry sections won't.

*Compiled 8 September 2026. General information, not legal advice. IRCC changes
forms, fees and processing times without notice — the personalised checklist
generated inside your own IRCC account is the only authoritative list.*

---

## Naming, once

Canada has **no "B-1 visa."** B-1 is a US classification. What you are applying
for is a **Temporary Resident Visa (TRV)**, form **IMM 5257**, declaring a
**business** purpose of travel. You are entering as a **business visitor** under
[IRPR s. 186(a)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-227/section-186.html)
/ [s. 187](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-227/section-187.html),
which requires **no work permit**. Searching for "Canada B-1" will get you
US-visa content and scraper sites.

You need a TRV because of **Indian citizenship**, not because of your F-1 status.
India is not on the partial-eTA list, so a valid US visa does not substitute.

---

## Plan the calendar backwards

IRCC's posted estimate from the US has run around 3 weeks in 2026; Indian
nationals routinely report longer. **Budget 12 weeks.** If your trip is fixed,
work back from it:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **T − 12 wk** | Decision gates (Phase 0). Talk to your DSO and your employer's immigration team |
| **T − 11 wk** | Request the invitation letter and the employer support letter — these take the longest to get out of other people |
| **T − 10 wk** | Gather financials and US-status documents. Create the IRCC account, generate your personalised checklist |
| **T − 9 wk** | Complete IMM 5257, upload everything, pay, submit |
| **T − 8 wk** | Biometrics at a USCIS ASC (the instruction letter typically gives you 30 days) |
| **T − 8 to −3 wk** | Processing. Watch the account for requests |
| *+4 to +8 wk* | *Only if a medical exam is requested — see the medical section* |
| **T − 3 wk** | Passport request letter → mail passport to the VAC |
| **T − 1 wk** | Passport returned with the visa foil. **Now** book non-refundable travel |
| **T − 0** | Travel, with the port-of-entry set in hand luggage |

## Phase 0 — Decide before you spend anything

Four gates. Any one of them can change the answer.

- [ ] **Is your F-1 visa stamp valid through your return date?**
      **Yes** → this is an ordinary trip; proceed.
      **No** → you would return under Automatic Visa Revalidation, and since
      6 Sep 2025 the fallback (renewing the stamp in Canada) is closed to
      third-country nationals. A refusal at the border means flying to India.
      Read the risk section before committing.
- [ ] **Do your activities qualify as a business visit?** Meetings,
      negotiations, conferences, site visits, B2B sales, intra-company training,
      contract-covered after-sales service — yes. Hands-on production or
      technical work, or a stay over six months — no, that is a work permit.
- [ ] **Have you spoken to your DSO?** They sign the I-20 travel endorsement and
      hold the current institutional position on travel risk.
- [ ] **Have you routed it through your employer's immigration team?** If your
      employer has one, this is their job and their counsel budget.

### Timing note — 15 September 2026

DHS's rule replacing duration of status with fixed admission periods takes effect
**15 Sep 2026**. F-1 students **present in the US on that date** continue in
status to the later of the I-20 program end date or the **STEM OPT EAD expiry**.
Re-entry on or after that date is on a **date-specific I-94**. A TRV cannot be
obtained before then, so plan on returning under the new regime — and do not let
a trip straddle the date.

## Phase 1 — The complete document set

Assemble everything before you open the application. Scan to **PDF**, one file
per checklist item, **under 4 MB each**. Name files plainly:
`Raju_Mahesh_EmployerLetter.pdf`.

### A. Identity and travel document

- [ ] **Passport** — valid well beyond your return date, with at least two blank pages
- [ ] Scan of the **bio-data page**
- [ ] Scans of **every page bearing a visa, entry stamp or extension** — including old passports if your travel history lives there
- [ ] **Digital photograph** — taken within the last 6 months, plain background, neutral expression. Online applications take a digital file (roughly 420 × 540 px minimum, 60 KB – 4 MB). Paper applications need two printed 35 × 45 mm photos

### B. Proof of your status in the United States

This is the section that replaces "proof of residence in your home country," and
for a US-based applicant it carries a lot of the file's weight.

- [ ] **Form I-20** — most recent, showing the STEM OPT recommendation, signed by you and your DSO
- [ ] **EAD card** — STEM OPT employment authorization document, **both sides**
- [ ] **I-94 record** — print the current record from the CBP website
- [ ] **F-1 visa stamp** — scan it even if expired; IRCC wants the history
- [ ] **SEVIS I-901 fee receipt** — optional, harmless to include

> Your US status must be valid when you apply. The more remaining validity it
> shows, the stronger the file — an officer is assessing whether you can and will
> leave Canada, and a nearly-expired EAD invites the question of whether you'd be
> able to return to the US at all. Apply while your documents have runway.

### C. Purpose of travel — the section that decides a business application

- [ ] **Invitation letter from the Canadian host company** — the single most important document. Template in [Appendix A](#appendix-a--invitation-letter-template). Ask for **two originals**: one uploaded, one carried at the border
- [ ] **Support letter from your US employer** — template in [Appendix B](#appendix-b--employer-support-letter-template)
- [ ] **Detailed itinerary / agenda** — dates, meetings, locations, who you are meeting
- [ ] Whichever of these apply:
  - [ ] Conference or trade-show **registration confirmation**
  - [ ] **Purchase orders, contracts, or MOUs** between the two companies
  - [ ] **Warranty or service agreement**, if the trip is after-sales service — this is what makes that category legal, so it is not optional
  - [ ] Letter from a **parent or affiliate company**, for intra-company training

### D. Financial capacity

- [ ] **Bank statements — last 4 to 6 months.** Official statements, not screenshots
- [ ] **Recent pay stubs** — last 3 months. These double as your proof that remuneration comes from outside Canada, which is one of the three statutory business-visitor tests
- [ ] **Most recent US tax return** (Form 1040) or W-2
- [ ] If the host or your employer is covering costs, the letter must **say so explicitly** — then your own funds matter less

### E. Ties and intent to leave Canada

- [ ] **Employment verification letter** — role, salary, start date, that your position continues after the trip (can be combined with the support letter in B/C)
- [ ] **Lease or mortgage statement** for your US residence
- [ ] Evidence of family, property or obligations in India or the US
- [ ] **Travel history** — prior visas and entries, especially clean US, Schengen or UK records

### F. Forms

| Form | What it is | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| **IMM 5257** | Application for Temporary Resident Visa | Everyone |
| **IMM 5645** *or* **IMM 5707** | Family Information | Everyone — which one depends on the visa office; your checklist will name it |
| **IMM 5476** | Use of a Representative | Only if a lawyer or consultant files for you |
| **IMM 5484** | Document Checklist | Paper applications only |

## Phase 2 — File the application online

### Step 1 — Generate your personalised checklist

Go to IRCC's [Come to Canada / check your eligibility](https://www.cic.gc.ca/ctc-vac/getting-started.asp)
tool and answer the questionnaire honestly: **purpose = business**, country of
citizenship = India, country of residence = United States.

It outputs a **personalised document checklist and a reference code**. That
checklist overrides anything in this file.

### Step 2 — Create your IRCC secure account

Register with a **GCKey** username/password or a **Sign-In Partner**. Keep the
credentials somewhere durable — you'll be back in this account for status
updates, the biometrics letter and the passport request.

Enter your personalised reference code to load the checklist into the account.

### Step 3 — Complete IMM 5257

**This is where most applications break.** The form is a dynamic PDF:

1. **Download it to your computer.** Do not fill it in the browser
2. **Open it in desktop Adobe Acrobat Reader.** Preview, Chrome's viewer and most
   browser PDF plugins will silently fail to generate the barcodes
3. Complete every field. Use `N/A` rather than leaving blanks
4. **Purpose of visit: Business.** Not tourism. Consistency between the form, the
   invitation letter and what you say at the border is what an officer checks
5. Click **Validate** at the end. The form generates a **barcode page** — if no
   barcodes appear, the form is not accepted
6. Save the validated copy and upload that one

Then complete **IMM 5645 / IMM 5707** (family information) the same way.

### Step 4 — Upload supporting documents

Match one file to each checklist slot. If two documents belong in one slot,
merge them into a single PDF. Where the checklist offers an optional field, use
it — a short explanatory letter is better than an officer guessing.

**Consider a cover letter (a "letter of explanation").** One page, plain
language, addressed to the visa officer: who you are, who you work for, what the
trip is, exact dates, who pays, and that you will return to the US to resume your
employment. It costs nothing and it frames the file.

### Step 5 — Pay

| Item | Fee (CAD) |
|---|---|
| Visitor visa, per person | **$100** |
| Biometrics, per person | **$85** |
| **Total, single applicant** | **$185** |

Pay by credit card inside the portal. **Save the receipt** — it goes in the file
and you may need it later.

### Step 6 — Submit

Confirm the declaration and submit. The account shows a submission confirmation
and your **application number** — record it.

## Phase 3 — Biometrics

1. **The Biometrics Instruction Letter (BIL)** appears in your account, usually
   within 24 hours of payment. It carries your IRCC number
2. **You must give biometrics within the window the BIL states** — typically
   **30 days**. Miss it and the application can be refused for non-compliance
3. **In the United States, Canadian biometrics are collected at select USCIS
   Application Support Centers (ASCs)** under an arrangement between USCIS and
   IRCC. Schedule the appointment following the instructions in your BIL
4. **Bring a printed copy of the BIL and your passport.** Enrolment staff will
   not collect biometrics without the BIL
5. Fingerprints and a photograph. It takes minutes
6. **Biometrics are valid for 10 years.** If you gave them for a previous
   Canadian application inside that window, check whether you're exempt — the
   portal usually tells you

## Phase 4 — Processing

- Track status in your IRCC account. Statuses move through submitted → biometrics
  received → in progress → decision made
- **Watch for requests.** IRCC may issue a request for additional documents or,
  rarely, a medical exam. These carry hard deadlines. Missing one is a refusal
- **Do not book non-refundable travel.** IRCC says so explicitly, and a booked
  ticket does not accelerate anything
- Medical exams and police certificates are not normally required for business
  visits under six months

## Medical exams and TB history

Business visits under six months **normally require no immigration medical exam
(IME)**. Two things can change that: the occupational/residency triggers, and
your own answers on IMM 5257.

### The form asks about tuberculosis directly

IMM 5257's Background Information section asks:

> *"Within the past two years, have you or a family member ever had tuberculosis
> of the lungs or been in close contact with a person with tuberculosis?"*

Note the two-year window and the words **"of the lungs."** Work out the dates
against your own diagnosis and treatment before you tick a box.

**If the answer is genuinely uncertain, tick yes and explain.** The asymmetry is
severe:

| You answer | If IRCC agrees | If IRCC disagrees |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes**, with documentation | Possible medical exam, some delay | — |
| **No** | Nothing | **Misrepresentation under IRPA s. 40 — five-year ban** |

A medical exam costs weeks. A misrepresentation finding costs five years and
follows you into every future application. Never round a medical history down.

### Treated TB is not inadmissibility

Medical inadmissibility for danger to public health under
[IRPA s. 38(1)(a)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/i-2.5/section-38.html)
targets **active, infectious** disease. A completed, documented course of
treatment is not that. In practice:

| Situation | Typical outcome |
|---|---|
| **Active pulmonary TB, untreated** | Inadmissible until treated and non-infectious |
| **Pulmonary TB, treatment completed, documented cure** | Admissible. Expect to prove it |
| **Extrapulmonary TB** (lymph node, bone, etc.) | Generally not infectious, not a public-health danger |
| **Latent TB infection (LTBI)** | Not TB disease. May attract *medical surveillance* — a condition to report to public health on arrival |

The practical cost is **time, not refusal**. Chest X-ray abnormalities are the
single most common cause of medical-exam delay, and a healed TB lesion leaves
scarring that a radiologist will flag. Well-organised documentation is what turns
that from an investigation into a footnote.

### Assemble the medical dossier before you file

Have these ready so that a request for information can be answered in days rather
than weeks:

- [ ] **Treating physician's letter** — diagnosis, site (pulmonary or extrapulmonary), drug regimen, start and completion dates, and an explicit statement that treatment was completed and you are **cured and non-infectious**
- [ ] **Treatment records** — the regimen and adherence history
- [ ] **Sputum culture results**, including conversion to negative, if pulmonary
- [ ] **Chest X-ray reports** — at diagnosis and post-treatment, so the change is visible
- [ ] **Certified English translations** of anything not already in English
- [ ] Public health department clearance or discharge letter, if one was issued

### If an exam is requested

- It must be done by an **IRCC-designated panel physician** — your own doctor cannot do it
- **Tell the panel physician about the TB history up front.** Withholding it is what causes delays, not the history itself
- Results typically reach IRCC within about 30 days of the appointment
- An IME is valid 12 months

**Add 4–8 weeks to the calendar if a medical exam enters the picture** — the
12-week plan becomes 16–20.

## Phase 5 — Passport submission

1. On approval, a **Passport Request letter (PPR)** appears in your account
2. Complete the **VAC consent / submission form** and book a submission slot or
   use the mail-in service through the Canada Visa Application Centre operator in
   the US (**VFS Global**). Check the current VFS Canada USA site for locations
   and whether courier submission is offered from your city
3. Send: **passport**, the **PPR letter**, the **VAC form**, and a **prepaid
   return courier envelope** if you are mailing
4. **You will be without your passport for a stretch of weeks.** Do not schedule
   other international travel across this window, and make sure it doesn't
   collide with a US filing that needs the document
5. The visa foil is placed and the passport is couriered back in a sealed
   envelope. **Check the foil immediately** — name spelling, validity dates,
   entries permitted

Only now book flights.

## Phase 6 — Travelling

A visa lets you *travel to* Canada. **The CBSA officer at the port of entry
decides admissibility** — and after IRCC's March 2026 officer instructions,
business visitors face more scrutiny of foreign employment and foreign-source pay.

### Carry in hand luggage — never checked

**For Canadian entry:**

- [ ] Passport with the TRV foil
- [ ] **Invitation letter — the second original**
- [ ] Employer support letter
- [ ] **Recent pay stub** — fastest possible proof of foreign-source pay
- [ ] Agenda, conference registration, or the service contract
- [ ] Return ticket
- [ ] Host's **24-hour contact details**

**For US re-entry — carry these on the same trip:**

- [ ] **STEM OPT EAD card**
- [ ] **I-20 with a travel signature under 6 months old** (on OPT it's 6 months, not 12)
- [ ] **Employment verification letter** — employer, E-Verify participation, title, start date, relation to your degree
- [ ] Recent **pay stubs**
- [ ] Copy of your signed **Form I-983** training plan
- [ ] Degree certificate or transcript
- [ ] DSO contact details, including after-hours

Be able to state in one sentence what you're doing in Canada, who pays you, and
when you leave — matching your documents. CBP questions OPT students about
employment because unemployment days count against status (90 days on
post-completion OPT, 150 total with the STEM extension; **travel does not pause
the clock**).

## Appendix A — Invitation letter template

*On the Canadian host company's letterhead, signed by an officer of that company.
Ask for two originals.*

```
[Canadian company letterhead]
[Date]

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada

RE: Letter of Invitation — Business Visitor — [Your Full Name]

To the Visa Officer,

[Canadian Company] invites Mr./Ms./Mx. [Full Name as in passport], born
[DD Month YYYY], a citizen of India holding passport [number], to visit our
offices at [full address] for business purposes.

RELATIONSHIP
[Your Employer] and [Canadian Company] have an ongoing business relationship
concerning [describe: supply agreement, joint project, service contract,
affiliate relationship]. [Name] is employed by [Your Employer] as
[Job Title].

PURPOSE OF VISIT
The purpose of this visit is [specific: technical design review of X; attending
the Y conference; negotiating the Z agreement; providing after-sales service
under contract dated DD Month YYYY]. The activities are limited to
[meetings / consultations / conference attendance / inspection], and the visit
is international in scope.

DATES
Arrival [DD Month YYYY], departure [DD Month YYYY] — [N] days.

EMPLOYMENT AND REMUNERATION
[Name] remains employed and remunerated exclusively by [Your Employer] in the
United States throughout this visit. [Name] will receive no salary, wages,
or other remuneration from [Canadian Company] or any Canadian source, and will
not enter the Canadian labour market.

EXPENSES
[Canadian Company / Your Employer] will be responsible for [airfare,
accommodation, local transport, meals].

CONTACT
[Host name], [title], [direct phone], [mobile — available 24 hours],
[email], [company address].

We would appreciate your favourable consideration.

Sincerely,
[Signature]
[Name], [Title]
[Canadian Company]
```

**Why the wording matters.** The remuneration paragraph is not boilerplate — it
is the evidence for two of the three statutory tests in IRPR 187(3). A letter
missing it is a materially weaker letter.

## Appendix C — Why business applications get refused

| Reason | What to do about it |
|---|---|
| Generic invitation letter with no named agenda | Specific purpose, specific dates, named host with a 24-hour number |
| No explicit "paid outside Canada, no Canadian funds" wording | Put it in both letters, verbatim |
| Officer not satisfied you'll leave Canada | US lease, employment continuity letter, EAD with runway, return ticket |
| Purpose inconsistent across form, letters and interview | One story. Check IMM 5257 says **Business** |
| Thin or unexplained finances | 4–6 months of official statements; explain any large recent deposit |
| IMM 5257 submitted without barcodes | Desktop Adobe Reader, click **Validate** |
| Biometrics window missed | Book the ASC appointment the day the BIL arrives |
| Activity is really work | Apply for a work permit instead |
| Medical history understated on the form | Answer accurately — misrepresentation is a 5-year ban |
| Applied three weeks before the trip | 12 weeks |
