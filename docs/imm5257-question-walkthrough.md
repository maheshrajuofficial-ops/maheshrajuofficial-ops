# IMM 5257 — Question-by-Question Walkthrough

**The order you'll actually meet the questions, and how to answer each one.**

> **Profile assumed:** Indian citizen, resident in the United States on **F-1
> STEM OPT**, travelling to Canada as a **business visitor** for under six
> months. Answers below are mapped to that profile — but every one of them is a
> statement about **your** facts, not a script. Where your history differs,
> your history wins.

*Compiled 8 September 2026. Form versions change; the wording in front of you
governs.*

---

## The two stages

| Stage | What it is | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Eligibility questionnaire** | IRCC's "Come to Canada" wizard, ~10 min | Personalised document checklist + **reference code** |
| **2. IMM 5257** | The application form itself, 10 sections | Validated PDF with barcodes |

Stage 1 shapes stage 2. Answer it as a **business visitor** and the checklist it
generates asks for business documents. Answer it as a tourist and you'll be
uploading the wrong things all the way through.

---

## Stage 1 — The eligibility questionnaire

Roughly this sequence. Wording shifts between releases; the substance doesn't.

| # | Question | Your answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What would you like to do in Canada? | **Visit** |
| 2 | How long do you plan to stay? | **Temporarily — less than 6 months** |
| 3 | What is your country of citizenship? | **India** |
| 4 | What is your current country of residence? | **United States** |
| 5 | Do you have valid status in your country of residence? | **Yes** |
| 6 | What is your immigration status there? | **Student** — F-1 remains a student status while on OPT |
| 7 | Date of birth | As on passport |
| 8 | Do you have a valid US Permanent Resident card (green card)? | **No** |
| 9 | **Why are you visiting Canada?** | **Business** — not tourism |
| 10 | Do you have a family member who is a Canadian citizen or PR aged 18+? | Usually **No** |
| 11 | Have you had a medical exam by an IRCC panel physician in the last 12 months? | Usually **No** |

**Output: a reference code.** It expires — typically 60 days — so start the
application while it's live. It loads your personalised checklist into your IRCC
account.

> **The one that matters most is #9.** "Business" is what makes the checklist ask
> for an invitation letter and an employer letter. It also has to match what
> IMM 5257 says and what you tell the officer at the border. One story,
> everywhere.

## Stage 2 — IMM 5257, in form order

Fill it in **desktop Adobe Acrobat Reader**. Not a browser. Use `N/A` rather
than leaving a field blank.

### 1. Personal Details

| Field | How to answer |
|---|---|
| UCI | Blank if you've never dealt with IRCC before; otherwise the number they gave you |
| Full name | **Exactly as printed on your passport** — no expansions, no reordering. If your passport has no given name, follow IRCC's instructions for that case rather than inventing one |
| Other names used | Yes/No honestly — maiden names, spelling variants on old documents |
| Sex, date of birth, place and country of birth | As on passport |
| Citizenship | **India** |
| **Current country of residence** | **United States**, status **Student**, with the **from / to** dates of your status |
| Previous countries of residence (6+ months in last 5 years) | **India**, if applicable, with dates |
| Country where applying | **United States** |

> **The status dates are a real decision.** Use dates your documents actually
> support — your I-94, I-20 or EAD. Since the fixed-admission rule took effect
> **15 September 2026**, a date-specific I-94 is the cleanest source. Whatever
> you enter, be able to point at the document it came from.

### 2. Language

Native language; whether you can communicate in English, French, both or
neither; which you prefer for correspondence; whether you've taken an approved
language test (for a visitor visa, almost always **No**).

### 3. Passport

Number, country of issue (**India**), issue and expiry dates. Copy them
character by character — a transposed digit here is a rejected application.

### 4. National Identity Document

India issues them, so **Yes**. Aadhaar or PAN, with the number and dates.

### 5. US PR Card

*"Are you a lawful permanent resident of the United States with a valid alien
registration card (green card)?"* → **No.**

Don't be tempted by this because you live in the US. It asks specifically about a
green card. An EAD is not one.

### 6. Contact Information

Current mailing address, residential address (say so if identical), telephone,
email. **Use an email you check daily** — this is where the biometrics
instruction letter and the passport request land, both on deadlines.

### 7. Details of Visit to Canada

| Field | How to answer |
|---|---|
| Purpose of visit | **Business** |
| From / To | Your actual intended dates |
| Funds available (CAD) | A realistic figure your bank statements support. Don't inflate it |
| Name of person or institution you'll visit | **The Canadian host company** |
| Relationship | **Business** |
| Address | The host's business address |

Consistency check: these dates and this host must match the invitation letter and
the itinerary you upload.

### 8. Education

Post-secondary only. Your bachelor's and your master's — institution, field,
city, country, from/to dates.

### 9. Employment

**Ten years, no gaps.** Work backwards from your current role. Every month must
be accounted for — periods of study go in as "Student," periods out of work as
"Unemployed." An unexplained gap invites a question you'd rather not answer.

Include roles held in India before you moved.

### 10. Background Information — the section that carries risk

Four groups. Answer each on your own facts.

#### Health

**1(a)** *"Within the past two years, have you or a family member ever had
tuberculosis of the lungs or been in close contact with a person with
tuberculosis?"*

Two independent limbs, both governed by the **two-year window**:

- **"tuberculosis of the lungs"** — pulmonary disease specifically. Purely
  extrapulmonary disease (chest wall, lymph node, bone, abdominal) is not
  tuberculosis of the lungs, and a record headed *extrapulmonary* has made that
  classification for you.
- **"or been in close contact with a person with tuberculosis"** — no site
  qualifier. Independently **yes** if someone around you had TB inside the
  window, whatever your own history.

Worked example: extrapulmonary disease, treatment completed in early 2024 →
**No**, on two separate grounds — wrong site, and outside a window that opens in
September 2024. Read the dates off the record, not off memory.

**1(b)** *"Do you have any physical or mental disorder that would require social
and/or health services, other than medication, during your stay in Canada?"*

This asks about **services you'd need while in Canada**, not your medical
history. A completed course of treatment with no ongoing care requirement is
**No**.

#### Immigration history — *the sleeper question*

**2(a)** *"Have you ever remained beyond the validity of your status, attended
school or worked without authorization in Canada?"*

**2(b)** *"Have you ever been refused a visa or permit, denied entry to, or
ordered to leave Canada or any other country or territory?"*

> **Read 2(b) twice.** It says **any country**, and it says **refused**. A US
> F-1 refusal under s. 214(b) — including one years ago, at your first attempt,
> before the approval that actually got you here — **is a refusal**, and it is a
> **yes**. So is a Schengen or UK refusal.
>
> A truthful yes is routine; officers see them constantly and a 214(b) at age 21
> decides nothing. A **no** that IRCC's records contradict is misrepresentation
> under IRPA s. 40 — **a five-year ban**. This question, not the medical one, is
> where most applications in your profile go wrong.

**2(c)** *"Have you previously applied to enter or remain in Canada?"*

#### Criminal history

**3** *"Have you ever committed, been arrested for, been charged with or
convicted of any criminal offence in any country or territory?"*

Note **"committed"** and **"arrested for"** — broader than "convicted." A DUI
counts. An arrest with charges dropped counts.

#### Military and affiliations

**4(a)** Service in any military, militia, civil defence unit, security
organisation or police force — including non-obligatory, reserve or volunteer
service.

**4(b)** Membership of or association with a political party or organisation
that has engaged in or advocated violence, or been involved in criminal
activity.

**4(c)** Having witnessed or participated in ill treatment of prisoners or
civilians, looting, or desecration of religious buildings.

**A yes in group 3 or 4 pulls in Schedule 1** (IMM 5257 Schedule 1) with the
details.

### 11. Consent, declaration, and Validate

Type your name as the signature, date it, then click **Validate**. The form
generates a **barcode page**. No barcodes means the form will not be accepted —
that almost always means it was filled in a browser rather than desktop Adobe
Reader.

## IMM 5707 — Family Information

Separate form, same care. Parents, spouse or partner, children, and **all
siblings** — including those who are deceased, estranged, or who have never
travelled. Mark deceased entries as such rather than omitting them.

Marital status must match what you entered on IMM 5257.

---

## Then, in the portal

1. Upload each document into its checklist slot
2. Answer the portal's own declaration questions
3. Pay — **CAD $100** visa + **CAD $85** biometrics
4. Submit, and record the application number

The **Biometrics Instruction Letter** normally appears within 24 hours. It starts
a clock, typically 30 days.

---

## The four consistency checks before you submit

Failures here are self-inflicted and entirely avoidable.

- [ ] **Purpose is "Business"** in the questionnaire, on IMM 5257, in the
      invitation letter, and in what you'll say at the border
- [ ] **Dates match** across form, invitation letter and itinerary
- [ ] **Funds declared** are supported by the bank statements you uploaded
- [ ] **Every "yes"** in Background Information has an explanation and, where
      required, a Schedule 1

## The three answers worth pausing on

| Question | Why it's not obvious |
|---|---|
| **Status in the US** | Student, not worker — F-1 stays a student status on OPT. Dates must trace to a real document |
| **US PR Card** | No. Living in the US isn't the same as holding a green card |
| **Ever refused a visa — any country** | Old US 214(b) refusals count. The single most common route into a misrepresentation finding |
